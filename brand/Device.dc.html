The logo teaches the reader one rule in the first second: solid is measured, dash-dot is a reference. That is free vocabulary, and the provenance rule needs exactly it. Below is what it looks like carried into the product — not replacing ≈, which stays, but giving it a visual partner so a derived number is distinguishable at a glance rather than on inspection.

A rule under the figure, not around it — no boxes, no tint, nothing that competes with the verdict colours.
In the chart it is already true
The pace line is the datum, the measured curve is solid, the ceiling is a plain hairline. The mark is a crop of this.
Two rules that keep it honest
Never dash-dot a measured figure — the device means reference or estimate, and lying with it costs more than it earns.

Never use dash-dot as decoration: no dashed dividers, borders or ornament anywhere in the product or the site. If it is dash-dot, it is making a claim about provenance.
